[Self-Improving Pretraining: using post-trained models to pretrain better models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21343)

Ellen Xiaoqing Tan, Jack Lanchantin, Shehzaad Dhuliawala, Danwei Li, Thao Nguyen, Jing Xu, Ping Yu, Ilia Kulikov, Sainbayar Sukhbaatar, Jason Weston, Xian Li, Olga Golovneva

> "Data wall"이 다가오는 상황에서, 이미 보유한 **강한 post-trained 모델**을 되돌려 다음 세대 pretraining 단계에서 (a) 원시 코퍼스의 suffix를 재작성하고 (b) 학생 정책의 롤아웃을 판정(judge)하게 만든다. Post-training 기법(RL, DPO, 합성 재작성)을 단일 파이프라인으로 **upstream으로 당긴** 공격적 구현. 1.4B continual pretraining에서 quality win rate 86.3%, factuality +36.2% 상대 개선, Thinking Mid-training에서 post-training 후 direct RL 대비 3.2× 향상.

2. safety/quality/factuality judge 점수가 reward summation 전에 어떻게 정규화·가중되는가, 그리고 task-specific fine-tuning이 있는 8B judge를 prompted GPT-OSS-120B judge가 84.3% vs 72.1%로 앞서는 이유는 무엇인가?

## Answers

**1.** Offline DPO on synthetic data typically collapses because the chosen/rejected pairs are fixed at the start, so the policy rapidly learns surface features that distinguish the teacher's style from its own — amounting to stylistic imitation rather than quality improvement. SIP's Online DPO refreshes the preference set every step by sampling K=1–16 fresh rollouts from the *current* policy, so gradients are always relative to the model's current failure modes. K=16 dominates K=1 precisely because more rollouts produce a wider coverage of near-miss negatives, denying the optimizer easy shortcut features. The K=1 SFT collapse reported in the ablation is the canonical evidence: with a single rollout and no contrastive pressure, the model regresses to teacher mimicry. The emergent 400–800 step transition, where policy rollouts begin winning the judge's preference against the teacher rewrite, is consistent with an implicit curriculum — early on the 1.4B student has no chance of outwriting an 8B teacher on the hardest suffixes, so gradients flow from {teacher > policy} pairs; once the student masters the "easy" distribution, the teacher's remaining edge shrinks and the judge starts preferring rollouts on prefixes where the teacher itself was mediocre. The paper reports this transition as observation rather than formally verifying a mechanistic cause — calling it an "automatic curriculum" is partly interpretive.

**2.** The paper describes reward summation across safety/quality/factuality but does not fully specify the normalization scheme; the natural reading is that each judge emits a bounded scalar (likely 0–1 or a fixed Likert range) and the rewards are summed unweighted, with the generality claim resting on this additivity. That under-specification is itself a limitation: unweighted summation implicitly assigns equal marginal value to a safety-point and a quality-point, which is rarely what a deployment wants. On the judge comparison, the 84.3% vs 72.1% gap for prompted GPT-OSS-120B over fine-tuned Llama3.1-8B likely reflects three compounding factors: (i) raw capacity — 120B parameters carry more world knowledge to ground factuality and nuanced quality calls; (ii) fine-tuning on a narrow judge corpus can overfit to surface heuristics of the training labels, losing the broad calibration a prompted frontier model retains; (iii) the 8B judge shares a family with the 8B rewriter/teacher, creating a mild circularity the paper itself flags. This matches the LLM-as-judge literature where scale and generality often beat specialization for open-ended evaluation — with the caveat that the 120B judge is also much more expensive per call.

**3.** In principle yes — the paper's framing pushes in that direction: a single 120B judge with a well-engineered prompt already produces a combined quality/safety/factuality signal that outperforms the specialized 8B fine-tuned judge on quality (84.3% vs 72.1%). Replacing the traditional cascade (MinHash dedup → classifier-based quality filter → toxicity filter → factuality check) would collapse dozens of small models and heuristics into one inference pass. The blocker is cost, not capability: prompted 120B inference on every candidate document in a multi-trillion-token corpus is orders of magnitude more expensive than hash-based and small-classifier steps. A rough envelope — 120B at ~240 GFLOPs per output token, a few hundred output tokens per judgment, applied across ~10^10 documents in a 10^12-token corpus — lands in the 10^22–10^23 FLOP range, comparable to training a mid-sized model itself; the paper does not report a compute-matched comparison at this level. The practical path is therefore hybrid: cheap filters for the bulk, frontier-judge adjudication on borderline or high-value data — which is effectively what SIP does by *rewriting* only suffixes flagged as unsafe/low-quality rather than judging every token.

**4.** The paper does not report cross-family judge re-evaluation or blinded human ratings, so the 86.3% quality win rate should be treated as upper-bound evidence contingent on the GPT-OSS-120B judge's preferences. Three decomposition concerns are live. First, judge-stylistic preference: the rewriter was GRPO-trained against this judge family, so rewrites likely exhibit the judge's preferred register (structure, hedging, length), inflating win rate without necessarily improving downstream task capability. Second, label leakage through family similarity — GPT-OSS-120B and the 8B teacher share design lineage a Claude or Gemini judge would not reproduce. Third, even if a cross-family judge confirmed the gap, "quality-judge preference" ≠ "factual capability"; the factuality win (42.3→57.6, +36.2% rel) is more directly verifiable because factuality admits ground-truth checks, whereas quality does not. A robust re-evaluation pairs a disjoint-family judge with targeted downstream benchmarks (TriviaQA for factuality, human raters for quality); the 8B from-scratch safety result (32.4% win vs 1.3% baseline) is the most credible number because the baseline is so low that stylistic confounds alone cannot explain the gap.

**5.** The paper explicitly acknowledges that no compute-matched baselines are reported, so the honest answer is we do not know from this work alone. The 16 rollouts per step + 120B judge calls + 8B rewriter inference likely make SIP 5–20× more expensive per token than standard next-token pretraining (exact ratio depends on rollout length and judge batching). Against the three comparisons: (i) longer standard pretraining on the same corpus is the hardest baseline because at matched FLOPs it simply sees more tokens — SIP's rewrite gains would need to exceed the log-linear scaling curve of data size; (ii) vanilla sequence-level distillation from the same 8B teacher is probably the closest competitor, and SIP's edge must come from the contrastive Online DPO signal over K rollouts rather than teacher knowledge transfer alone — the K=1 SFT collapse is suggestive but not conclusive; (iii) rejection-sampling SFT (generate K, keep best by judge, SFT on it) is arguably the most compute-similar baseline and is notably absent from the ablation table. The 8B/21K-step ceiling also leaves open whether gains compound or saturate at larger scales — the RL-for-pretraining literature has cases where small-scale wins do not extrapolate.

**6.** Exact-match reward on a 128-token suffix is catastrophically sparse because the space of valid continuations is vastly larger than the single ground-truth string — the model rarely gets positive signal, the gradient collapses, and thinking degenerates into prefix that ignores the context. Log-probability on ground truth under a fixed reference model is denser but introduces its own pathology: the optimal "thinking" becomes whatever linguistic prefix most boosts the reference model's logprob of the fixed suffix — rewarding priming and memorization cues rather than genuine reasoning. Semantic-similarity reward from GPT-OSS-120B sidesteps both failure modes by giving partial credit for continuations that match the *meaning* but not the surface form, providing a dense, continuous reward surface that DrGRPO's bias correction can actually optimize against. The 3.2× improvement after post-training (0.3785 vs 0.1197 direct RL on base) and 9× over raw-data RL support that the reward shape — not just the RL stage — is what unlocks gains. The risk, flagged by the paper as "judge circularity," is that the student now optimizes for whatever semantic-equivalence heuristic the 120B judge uses, so apparent reasoning gains may partly reflect alignment with the judge's embedding geometry rather than absolute capability improvement — cross-family judge validation would disambiguate this.

**7.** The paper sits at the intersection of three Knowledge2Deck threads and is best understood as their synthesis. Versus **RLP (Reinforcement as a Pretraining Objective)**: RLP established that RL signals can be applied during pretraining using next-token or verifier-based rewards with internal or programmatic reward sources; SIP's novelty is (a) using a *separate strong post-trained model* as the reward source — effectively bootstrapping the next generation's pretraining from last generation's post-training artifacts — and (b) combining *data rewriting* with *policy RL* in a single loop, so the pretraining corpus improves alongside the policy. Thinking Mid-training's semantic-similarity reward via frontier judge is a stronger reward shaping than RLP's logprob-style signals. Versus **Demystifying Synthetic Data in LLM Pre-training**: that line characterizes when synthetic data helps or hurts (diversity, distribution match, contamination); SIP operationalizes one specific recipe — selective rewriting gated by a judge, plus contrastive preference training — and the "pure synthetic collapse" failure mode documented there is addressed by keeping the *original* data as the anchor in every preference triplet. Versus the broader **"post-training moved upstream"** trend (instruction-tuning mixed into pretraining, RLHF-style signals earlier in the curriculum, the "mid-training" category generally): SIP is an aggressive instance, essentially arguing that if you already have a strong post-trained model, you should amortize its value by using it as both data-author and reward-model for the next pretrain. What it adds that RLP does not: (1) explicit external strong-model supervision rather than self-play or rule-based rewards, (2) the Online DPO + K-rollout contrastive setup that prevents teacher-mimicry collapse of naive distillation, and (3) a multi-axis reward-summation framework (safety+quality+factuality) that generalizes beyond a single scalar. Shared limitations it does not escape: judge circularity (same-family risk), absence of compute-matched baselines, and uncertainty about whether these gains survive scaling beyond 8B — all weaknesses common to much of the mid-training literature.
