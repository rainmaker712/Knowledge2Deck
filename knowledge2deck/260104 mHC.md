[mHC: Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connections](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23852)

Authors: Zhenda Xie, Yixuan Wei, Huanqi Cao, Chenggang Zhao, Chengqi Deng, Jiashi Li, Damai Dai, Huazuo Gao, Jiang Chang, Liang Zhao, Shangyan Zhou, Zhean Xu, Zhengyan Zhang, Wangding Zeng, Shengding Hu, Yuqing Wang, Jingyang Yuan, Lean Wang, Wenfeng Liang

> Recently, studies exemplified by Hyper-Connections (HC) have extended the ubiquitous residual connection paradigm established over the past decade by expanding the residual stream width and diversifying connectivity patterns. While yielding substantial performance gains, this diversification fundamentally compromises the identity mapping property intrinsic to the residual connection, which causes severe training instability and restricted scalability, and additionally incurs notable memory access overhead. To address these challenges, we propose Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connections (mHC), a general framework that projects the residual connection space of HC onto a specific manifold to restore the identity mapping property, while incorporating rigorous infrastructure optimization to ensure efficiency. Empirical experiments demonstrate that mHC is effective for training at scale, offering tangible performance improvements and superior scalability. We anticipate that mHC, as a flexible and practical extension of HC, will contribute to a deeper understanding of topological architecture design and suggest promising directions for the evolution of foundational models.

## Follow-Up Questions

1. Why does expanding the residual stream width in Hyper-Connections (HC) inherently compromise the identity mapping property, and what specific training instabilities arise as a consequence?

2. How does projecting the residual connection space onto a manifold restore the identity mapping property while still preserving the performance benefits of diversified connectivity patterns?

3. What is the relationship between the identity mapping property and the scalability of a model during training? Why would losing this property restrict how large the model can be trained?

4. The paper mentions "notable memory access overhead" in HC. What structural choices in HC cause this overhead, and how does mHC's infrastructure optimization address it without sacrificing the richer connectivity?

5. How would you compare mHC's approach to topological architecture design against other architectural innovations like attention mechanisms or gating functions in terms of what problem each is solving in the information flow of deep networks?

6. What are the limitations of constraining connections to a specific manifold - are there expressivity trade-offs, and under what training regimes or model scales might those trade-offs become significant?

7. The authors frame mHC as contributing to "topological architecture design." What future architectural directions does this framing suggest, and how might manifold constraints be generalized beyond residual connections to other components of a transformer?
